Version: 0.1.0
Date: 2026-08-25
  Circuit Network:
    - The plasma temperature signal now reads in THOUSANDS of degrees Celsius. A reactor at 600
      million degrees used to put 600000000 on the wire and now puts 600000. Any decider or
      arithmetic combinator comparing this signal against a fixed number needs that number divided
      by 1000.
    - Why: a circuit signal is a 32-bit number and stops at 2147483647, which is colder than a
      deuterium-tritium plasma actually runs. The old scale meant the hottest reactors all reported
      the same figure no matter how hot they really were. This does not fix that on its own -- the
      temperature limit has not moved yet, so the hottest reactors still read alike -- but it is
      what has to happen first, and it lifts the limit that was stopping it.
    - A reactor below 500 degrees now reports 0, the same as one holding no plasma. The status line
      under the reactor still tells the two apart.
    - The plasma's own tooltip, on the pipe, still reads in whole degrees. It will look a thousand
      times larger than the wire. The wire is the number to write conditions against.
