Version: 0.1.0
Date: 2026-08-25
  Circuit Network:
    - The plasma temperature signal now reads in THOUSANDS of degrees Celsius. A reactor at 600
      million degrees used to put 600000000 on the wire and now puts 600000. Any decider or
      arithmetic combinator comparing this signal against a fixed number needs that number divided
      by 1000.
    - Why: a circuit signal is a 32-bit number and stops at 2147483647, which is colder than a
      deuterium-tritium plasma actually runs. The old scale meant the hottest reactors all reported
      the same figure no matter how hot they really were. This does not fix that on its own -- the
      temperature limit has not moved yet, so the hottest reactors still read alike -- but it is
      what has to happen first, and it lifts the limit that was stopping it.
    - A reactor below 500 degrees now reports 0, the same as one holding no plasma. The status line
      under the reactor still tells the two apart.
    - The plasma's own tooltip, on the pipe, still reads in whole degrees. It will look a thousand
      times larger than the wire. The wire is the number to write conditions against.
  Balancing:
    - Deuterium-tritium reactors now run hotter and are no longer stuck at one temperature. The
      limit that was holding them there has moved from 2 billion to 5 billion degrees, so a D-T
      reactor settles where its own physics puts it -- about 3.25 billion degrees, rising to 3.9
      billion with the confinement research done -- and the temperature you read now moves with the
      reactor instead of sitting still.
    - Its Q factor reads LOWER as a result, about 73 instead of 96. The reactor is not worse; it was
      being flattered by a limit that stopped it before its output fell off. Nothing about what it
      produces got smaller.
    - Deuterium-helium-3 gives a little back, Q about 19 instead of 21, because the extra
      temperature carries it past the point its reaction works best. That is the physics and it is
      not a nerf aimed at the tier.
    - Deuterium-deuterium reactors are unchanged at any normal supply. If you deliberately run one
      very thin -- around a tenth of a box -- it was already sitting against the old limit, so it
      now runs hotter like the others do. Supplied normally, nothing about the first tier moved.
    - Helium-3 against itself is still not worth building. Raising the limit was expected to rescue
      it and does not: radiation, not the limit, is what stops that reaction. Its best case improves
      from about 0.018 to 0.032 times break-even, which is not a tier becoming worth reaching.
